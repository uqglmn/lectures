  • Part I culminates in quantum mechanics. We make precise what it means for a group to be a symmetry of a Hamiltonian system — classically and quantum mechanically — and harvest the consequences: energy levels organise into symmetry-enforced multiplets whose sizes are dimensions of irreducible representations, while reduced-eigenvalue multiplicities and reality constraints determine total degeneracy; perturbations that lower the symmetry split the levels according to character restriction; and transition amplitudes obey selection rules governed by the Wigner–Eckart theorem, whose engine is tensor products and their Clebsch–Gordan decomposition. The infrared rule of Lecture 6 is derived, and invariant tensors open the bridge to Part II.
