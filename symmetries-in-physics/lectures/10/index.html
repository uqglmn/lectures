01The group, the algebra, and the two dictionaries

The calculation this lecture performs — complexify, find a highest weight, descend by ladders, count — is the model for every representation theory Part II will do. We first lay out the cast.

lemmaComplexification is legal

Let VVV be a finite-dimensional complex vector space. Every representation π\piπ of the real Lie algebra su(2)\su(2)su(2) on VVV extends uniquely to a complex-linear representation πC\pi_{\C}πC​ of sl(2,C)=su(2)⊕i su(2)\sllie(2,\C)=\su(2)\oplus\ii\,\su(2)sl(2,C)=su(2)⊕isu(2), namely πC(X+iY)=π(X)+i π(Y),X,Y∈su(2),\pi_{\C}(X+\ii Y)=\pi(X)+\ii\,\pi(Y), \qquad X,Y\in\su(2),πC​(X+iY)=π(X)+iπ(Y),X,Y∈su(2), and every complex-linear representation of sl(2,C)\sllie(2,\C)sl(2,C) arises this way. In particular π\piπ is irreducible if and only if πC\pi_{\C}πC​ is.

05The quantum theory of angular momentum and spin

A quantum system is rotationally symmetric when rotations act on its Hilbert space by unitaries commuting with the Hamiltonian — Lecture 9’s quantum Noether setting. Acting by which group? The geometric answer is SO(3)\SO(3)SO(3); the group this lecture has classified is SU(2)\SU(2)SU(2). The discrepancy is real, productive, and deliberately deferred: quantum mechanics cares only about rays, and Lecture 11 will prove that ray-level representations of SO(3)\SO(3)SO(3) are precisely ordinary representations of SU(2)\SU(2)SU(2). For now we record the IOU and take the symmetry group to be SU(2)\SU(2)SU(2), noting that at the level of observables there is nothing to choose: su(2)≅so(3)\su(2)\cong\so(3)su(2)≅so(3) (Lectures 8 and 9), and the Hermitian generators obey [Ja,Jb]=i ϵabcJc[J_a,J_b]=\ii\,\epsilon_{abc}J_c[Ja​,Jb​]=iϵabc​Jc​ either way.
