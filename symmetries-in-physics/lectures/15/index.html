The Poincaré group and the classification of particles

  • The finale executes the program. Adding translations to the Lorentz group gives the Poincaré group — a semidirect product in the sense of Lecture 2, realised as 5×55\times55×5 matrices — and the fields-versus-states principle of Lecture 14 says that its infinite-dimensional unitary irreducible representations are the elementary particles. We classify them by Wigner's method: diagonalise the translations, watch momenta sweep out orbits — mass shells and the light cone, with their invariant measures derived separately — and reduce everything to the little group of one reference momentum: SU(2)\SU(2)SU(2) for massive particles, whence spin; the double cover of the Euclidean plane group for massless ones, whence helicity and the photon's two polarisations, alongside the mathematically allowed continuous-spin branch. For massive particles the Casimirs recover mass and spin; for finite-helicity massless particles both Casimirs vanish and helicity remains a little-group character. A one-page retrospect closes the course by stating its boundary: the representation-theoretic vocabulary needed for SU(3)\SU(3)SU(3) has been prepared, while particle-physics SU(3)\SU(3)SU(3) itself is assigned to explicit further reading.
