The central thread

A vector space is a set closed under addition and scalar multiplication and obeying eight axioms; what the vectors “are'' is irrelevant. Coordinate nnn-tuples, matrices, polynomials, functions, and quantum states are all examples. A subspace is a subset that is closed under both operations and contains 000—check exactly those three things. Subspaces combine through sums, and a sum is direct (decompositions are unique) precisely when the subspaces meet only at 000. Keep C\CC, not R\RR, as the default field: quantum mechanics demands it.

Learn the argument

From familiar coordinates to abstract vector spaces

The examples look different, but the same closure rules govern them. Learn to test structure before reaching for coordinates.

Recognise the algebraic structure

Start with the field and linear-combination rules, then distinguish a subspace from an affine-looking impostor.

definition
Field

A field F\FF is a set with two operations, addition and multiplication, that are both commutative and associative and are linked by the distributive law a(b+c)=ab+aca(b+c)=ab+aca(b+c)=ab+ac. Each operation has an identity element (000 and 111, with 1≠01\neq01=0), every element has an additive inverse, and every nonzero element has a multiplicative inverse—so we may divide by any nonzero scalar.

Misconception check

“Every line or plane is a subspace.”

What geometric feature must every non-empty subspace possess?

Reveal the test

It must pass through the origin. That is necessary, though closure still has to be checked.

Key concepts5 ideas to retain
  • 01Field—scalars you can add, multiply, and divide by; here always R\RR or C\CC.
  • 02Vector space—a set closed under addition and scalar multiplication satisfying eight axioms—the nature of the vectors is irrelevant.
  • 03Linear combination—a1v1+⋯+amvma_1v_1+\cdots+a_mv_ma1​v1​+⋯+am​vm​; closure under these is the superposition principle.
  • 04Subspace—a subset containing 000 and closed under both operations (the three-part test).
  • 05Sum and direct sum—U1+⋯+UmU_1+\cdots+U_mU1​+⋯+Um​; direct (⊕\dsum⊕) when every vector decomposes uniquely, equivalently when 000 does.
