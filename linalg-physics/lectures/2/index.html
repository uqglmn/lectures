After the lecture

Concept summary

The span of a list is the smallest subspace containing it. A list is independent when 000 has only the trivial representation, and a basis is an independent spanning list—equivalently, a list giving every vector unique coordinates. Every linearly independent list is no longer than any spanning list (see the lecture notes); hence all bases share a length, the dimension. In an nnn-dimensional space, an independent or spanning list of length nnn is automatically a basis. Fixing an ordered basis turns each vector into a coordinate column in Fn\F^nFn, the operation a physicist calls choosing a representation. Finally, dim⁡(U+W)=dim⁡U+dim⁡W−dim⁡(U∩W)\dim(U+W)=\dim U+\dim W-\dim(U\cap W)dim(U+W)=dimU+dimW−dim(U∩W), with equality to dim⁡U+dim⁡W\dim U+\dim WdimU+dimW exactly for direct sums.
  • Span—all linear combinations of a list; the smallest subspace containing it.
  • Linear independence—only the trivial combination yields 000.
  • Basis—an independent spanning list; gives every vector unique coordinates.
  • Dimension—the common length of all bases (independent ≤\le≤ spanning).
  • Coordinate vector—the column [v]B∈Fn[v]_{\mathcal B}\in\F^n[v]B​∈Fn once an ordered basis is fixed—a physicist's representation.
  • Dimension formula—dim⁡(U+W)=dim⁡U+dim⁡W−dim⁡(U∩W)\dim(U+W)=\dim U+\dim W-\dim(U\cap W)dim(U+W)=dimU+dimW−dim(U∩W); additive exactly for direct sums.
