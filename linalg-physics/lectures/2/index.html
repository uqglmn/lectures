The central thread

The span of a list is the smallest subspace containing it. A list is independent when 000 has only the trivial representation, and a basis is an independent spanning list—equivalently, a list giving every vector unique coordinates. Every linearly independent list is no longer than any spanning list (the cited result); hence all bases share a length, the dimension. In an nnn-dimensional space, an independent or spanning list of length nnn is automatically a basis. Fixing an ordered basis turns each vector into a coordinate column in Fn\F^nFn; in a finite/discrete model this is what a physicist calls choosing a representation. Continuous position and momentum representations instead produce wavefunctions and require the later Hilbert-space framework. Finally, dim⁡(U+W)=dim⁡U+dim⁡W−dim⁡(U∩W)\dim(U+W)=\dim U+\dim W-\dim(U\cap W)dim(U+W)=dimU+dimW−dim(U∩W), with equality to dim⁡U+dim⁡W\dim U+\dim WdimU+dimW exactly for direct sums.

Learn the argument

From spanning lists to coordinates

A basis balances two demands: enough vectors to reach everything, but no redundancy. Dimension records the resulting count.

Misconception check

“A spanning list is automatically a basis.”

What goes wrong when one vector is already a combination of the others?

Reveal the test

The list still spans, but coordinates are no longer unique. A basis must also be linearly independent.
