The central thread

An inner product is sesquilinear (conjugate-linear in the first slot, linear in the second, in the physics convention), conjugate-symmetric, and positive definite; in Dirac notation ⟨ϕ|ψ⟩\braket{\phi}{\psi}⟨ϕ∣ψ⟩ is linear in the ket and conjugate-linear in the bra. It induces a norm ∥v∥=⟨v,v⟩\norm v=\sqrt{\inner v v}∥v∥=⟨v,v⟩​, orthogonality ⟨u,v⟩=0\inner u v=0⟨u,v⟩=0, and the Pythagorean theorem. The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality ∣⟨u,v⟩∣≤∥u∥∥v∥\abs{\inner u v}\le\norm u\norm v∣⟨u,v⟩∣≤∥u∥∥v∥ follows from orthogonal decomposition and bounds every amplitude; from it come the triangle inequality and, with the parallelogram law and polarization identity, the full geometry of the space. In physics, states are unit vectors, ∣⟨ϕ|ψ⟩∣2\abs{\braket\phi\psi}^2∣⟨ϕ∣ψ⟩∣2 is a probability, and orthogonality means distinguishability—the setting in which the spectral theorem will live.

Norm and orthogonality

The norm of vvv is ∥v∥=⟨v,v⟩\norm{v}=\sqrt{\inner{v}{v}}∥v∥=⟨v,v⟩​. A vector with ∥v∥=1\norm{v}=1∥v∥=1 is a unit vector (a normalized state). Two vectors are orthogonal, u⊥vu\perp vu⊥v, if ⟨u,v⟩=0\inner{u}{v}=0⟨u,v⟩=0.

Cauchy–Schwarz

For all u,vu,vu,v in an inner product space, ∣⟨u,v⟩∣≤∥u∥ ∥v∥,\abs{\inner{u}{v}}\le\norm u\,\norm v,∣⟨u,v⟩∣≤∥u∥∥v∥, with equality if and only if u,vu,vu,v are linearly dependent.

Misconception check

“The complex inner product is bilinear.”

What would happen to ⟨iv,iv⟩\langle iv,iv\rangle⟨iv,iv⟩ if no conjugation appeared in one slot?

Reveal the test

It could become negative. Conjugate linearity in one slot ensures ⟨v,v⟩\langle v,v\rangle⟨v,v⟩ is real and non-negative.
