The central thread

The tensor product V⊗WV\tsr WV⊗W has basis {ei⊗fj}\set{e_i\tsr f_j}{ei​⊗fj​} and dimension (dim⁡V)(dim⁡W)(\dim V)(\dim W)(dimV)(dimW); its elements are sums of elementary tensors v⊗wv\tsr wv⊗w, most of which do not factor, and the canonical bilinear map is characterized by the universal factorization property. In coordinates this is the Kronecker product, with (A⊗B)(v⊗w)=(Av)⊗(Bw)(A\tsr B)(v\tsr w)=(Av)\tsr(Bw)(A⊗B)(v⊗w)=(Av)⊗(Bw), tr⁡(A⊗B)=tr⁡A tr⁡B\tr(A\tsr B)=\tr A\,\tr Btr(A⊗B)=trAtrB, and eigenvalues λμ\lambda\muλμ over C\CC (or after complexification). In finite dimension, a type-(p,q)(p,q)(p,q) tensor is a multilinear map with ppp upper and qqq lower indices, unifying vectors, covectors, the metric, and operators (L(V,W)≅W⊗V∗\Lin(V,W)\iso W\tsr V^*L(V,W)≅W⊗V∗); a real metric is bilinear, while a complex inner product is Hermitian sesquilinear. Contraction over a matched pair generalizes the trace. As a bridge, symmetric and antisymmetric tensors split V⊗VV\tsr VV⊗V and model total exchange symmetry. In quantum mechanics finite composite pure-state vectors live in HA⊗HB\Hil_A\tsr\Hil_BHA​⊗HB​, and a nonzero pure state is entangled exactly when its ket does not factor. General Hilbert composites use the completed tensor product; mixed separability means a convex combination of product density operators.

Learn the argument

Combine systems without collapsing their structure

The canonical bilinear map creates elementary tensors; the universal property explains why every bilinear construction factors through them. Finite tensor types, contractions, and pure-state rank tests then share one language.

definition
Product and entangled states

A nonzero pure state of HA⊗HB\Hil_A\tsr\Hil_BHA​⊗HB​ is a product state if a representing ket factors as ∣ψ⟩⊗∣ϕ⟩\ket\psi\tsr\ket\phi∣ψ⟩⊗∣ϕ⟩, and entangled otherwise. Misconception check

“Every vector in $V\otimes W$ is a single product $v\otimes w$.”

Why does a sum of elementary tensors need not itself factor, and where does the zero tensor sit in the rank test?

Reveal the test

Decomposable tensors form a special nonlinear subset. Rank greater than one rules out factorization; rank at most one characterizes algebraic decomposability. Rank one is correct only after excluding zero, as for normalized pure states.
