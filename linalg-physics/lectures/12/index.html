Tensors and the Tensor Product

The canonical bilinear map creates elementary tensors; the universal property explains why every bilinear construction factors through them. Finite tensor types, contractions, and pure-state rank tests then share one language.

Lecture 11 introduced the indices; this lecture builds the objects they live on. The tensor product V⊗WV\tsr WV⊗W is the vector space whose elements combine a vector from VVV with one from WWW multiplicatively, and tensors of higher type unify vectors, covectors, operators, and bilinear forms into one framework. Its matrix incarnation is the Kronecker product, and its physical meaning is decisive: finite composite pure-state vectors use the tensor product of the parts, and a nonzero pure state is entangled precisely when it fails to factor. This closes Part II of the course.

remarkWhy this mattersOpen the observation

This lecture’s core uses finite-dimensional algebraic tensor products. General composite Hilbert spaces use the completed Hilbert tensor product HA⊗^HB\Hil_A\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}\Hil_BHA​⊗​HB​. The non-factorization criterion below is for nonzero pure states; a mixed state is separable when it is a convex combination of product density operators.

01The tensor product

A bilinear map V×W→UV\times W\to UV×W→U is awkward—it is not linear. The tensor product repackages it as an ordinary linear map out of a single space.

02The Kronecker product

In coordinates the tensor product is the Kronecker product: stacking the basis ei⊗fje_i\tsr f_jei​⊗fj​ lexicographically, the column of v⊗wv\tsr wv⊗w is the Kronecker product of the columns of vvv and www, and an operator A⊗BA\tsr BA⊗B has the block form A⊗B=(A11B⋯A1nB⋮⋮An1B⋯AnnB),A\tsr B=\begin{pmatrix}A_{11}B&\cdots&A_{1n}B\\ \vdots&&\vdots\\ A_{n1}B&\cdots&A_{nn}B\end{pmatrix},A⊗B=​A11​B⋮An1​B​⋯⋯​A1n​B⋮Ann​B​​, each entry AijA_{ij}Aij​ of AAA replaced by the block AijBA_{ij}BAij​B.

Tensor products of normal operators are normal on finite inner-product spaces.

03Tensors as multilinear maps

Throughout this section V,WV,WV,W are finite-dimensional. A tensor of type (p,q)(p,q)(p,q) is a multilinear map taking ppp covectors and qqq vectors to a scalar; equivalently it is an element of V⊗p⊗(V∗)⊗qV^{\tsr p}\tsr(V^*)^{\tsr q}V⊗p⊗(V∗)⊗q, with components τi1⋯ipj1⋯jq\tau^{i_1\cdots i_p}{}_{j_1\cdots j_q}τi1​⋯ip​j1​⋯jq​​ carrying ppp upper and qqq lower indices. The familiar objects are low-rank tensors: a vector is (1,0)(1,0)(1,0), a covector (0,1)(0,1)(0,1), a real bilinear form is (0,2)(0,2)(0,2), and a linear operator is (1,1)(1,1)(1,1).
