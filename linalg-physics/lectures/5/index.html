Lecture 5

Eigenvalues and Eigenvectors

4 learning objectives5 topics24 exercises

Before the lecture

What you will learn

  • Identify invariant subspaces, eigenvalues, eigenvectors, and eigenspaces.
  • Compute eigenvalues from the characteristic polynomial and recover tr⁡\trtr and det⁡\detdet.
  • Explain why every operator over C\CC has an eigenvalue (and may have none over R\RR).
  • Show eigenvectors for distinct eigenvalues are independent; triangularise over C\CC.

After the lecture

Concept summary

An eigenvector is a direction an operator only rescales, and the scale factor is its eigenvalue; the eigenspace E(λ,T)=null⁡(T−λI)E(\lambda,T)=\nul(T-\lambda I)E(λ,T)=null(T−λI) collects them, and λ\lambdaλ is an eigenvalue exactly when T−λIT-\lambda IT−λI fails to be invertible. Eigenvectors for distinct eigenvalues are independent, so an nnn-dimensional space admits at most nnn eigenvalues, and nnn distinct ones force a diagonalizing basis. Over C\CC every operator has an eigenvalue—the fundamental theorem of algebra in disguise—while over R\RR it may have none. The characteristic polynomial χA(λ)=det⁡(λI−A)\chi_A(\lambda)=\det(\lambda I-A)χA​(λ)=det(λI−A) has the eigenvalues as its roots, is a similarity invariant, and yields tr⁡A=∑λk\tr A=\sum\lambda_ktrA=∑λk​, det⁡A=∏λk\det A=\prod\lambda_kdetA=∏λk​. Finally, over C\CC every operator is upper-triangularizable, and then its eigenvalues are simply the diagonal entries.
  • Eigenvalue, eigenvector, eigenspace—Tv=λvTv=\lambda vTv=λv; E(λ,T)=null⁡(T−λI)E(\lambda,T)=\nul(T-\lambda I)E(λ,T)=null(T−λI); λ\lambdaλ is an eigenvalue   ⟺  T−λI\iff T-\lambda I⟺T−λI is not invertible.
  • Independence of eigenvectors—distinct eigenvalues give independent eigenvectors; at most dim⁡V\dim VdimV of them.
  • Existence over C\CC—every operator on a complex space has an eigenvalue (fundamental theorem of algebra).
  • Characteristic polynomial—χA(λ)=det⁡(λI−A)\chi_A(\lambda)=\det(\lambda I-A)χA​(λ)=det(λI−A); its roots are the eigenvalues, with tr⁡A=∑λk\tr A=\sum\lambda_ktrA=∑λk​, det⁡A=∏λk\det A=\prod\lambda_kdetA=∏λk​.
  • Upper-triangular form—exists over C\CC; the eigenvalues are then the diagonal entries.
  • Generalized eigenvectors—the generalized-eigenspace decomposition behind Jordan form (self-study).
