The central thread

An orthonormal basis satisfies ⟨ej,ek⟩=δjk\inner{e_j}{e_k}=\delta_{jk}⟨ej​,ek​⟩=δjk​; in it coordinates are inner products, v=∑k⟨ek,v⟩ekv=\sum_k\inner{e_k}{v}e_kv=∑k​⟨ek​,v⟩ek​, the norm obeys Parseval ∥v∥2=∑k∣⟨ek,v⟩∣2\norm v^2=\sum_k\abs{\inner{e_k}{v}}^2∥v∥2=∑k​∣⟨ek​,v⟩∣2, and ∑k∣ek⟩⟨ek∣=I\sum_k\ketbra{e_k}{e_k}=I∑k​∣ek​⟩⟨ek​∣=I. The Gram–Schmidt procedure builds such a basis from any basis, preserving partial spans, and shows every finite-dimensional inner product space has an ONB (and every orthonormal list extends to one). The orthogonal projection PUv=∑k⟨ek,v⟩ekP_Uv=\sum_k\inner{e_k}{v}e_kPU​v=∑k​⟨ek​,v⟩ek​ gives the orthogonal decomposition V=U⊕U⊥V=U\oplus U^\perpV=U⊕U⊥ and solves the best-approximation problem, with Bessel's inequality as a corollary. The Riesz theorem represents every linear functional as ⟨w,⋅⟩\inner{w}{\cdot}⟨w,⋅⟩, which is precisely the duality between bras and kets.

definition
Orthonormal list and basis

A list e1,…,eme_1,\dots,e_me1​,…,em​ is orthonormal if ⟨ej,ek⟩=δjk\inner{e_j}{e_k}=\delta_{jk}⟨ej​,ek​⟩=δjk​ (each is a unit vector, distinct ones orthogonal). An orthonormal list that is also a basis is an orthonormal basis (ONB).

Gram–Schmidt

Misconception check

“Projection chooses the nearest basis vector.”

What does projection onto a subspace combine when that subspace has several orthonormal basis vectors?

Reveal the test

It sums every orthogonal component ⟨ek,v⟩ek\langle e_k,v\rangle e_k⟨ek​,v⟩ek​. The answer is generally not one of the basis vectors.

A projective measurement

A spin in state ∣ψ⟩=12∣0⟩+12∣1⟩\ket\psi=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}\ket0+\tfrac1{\sqrt2}\ket1∣ψ⟩=2​1​∣0⟩+2​1​∣1⟩, measured in the {∣0⟩,∣1⟩}\set{\ket0,\ket1}{∣0⟩,∣1⟩} basis, yields ∣0⟩\ket0∣0⟩ with probability ∣⟨0,ψ⟩∣2=12\abs{\inner0\psi}^2=\tfrac12∣⟨0,ψ⟩∣2=21​ and ∣1⟩\ket1∣1⟩ with probability 12\tfrac1221​. The post-measurement state is the normalized projection onto the observed eigenspace—here ∣0⟩\ket0∣0⟩ or ∣1⟩\ket1∣1⟩. Measurement is orthogonal projection followed by renormalization.
