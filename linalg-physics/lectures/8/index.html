The central thread

Here VVV is finite-dimensional over F∈{R,C}\F\in\set{\R,\C}F∈{R,C}, and every basis sum is finite. ONB coordinates, Parseval, identity resolution, and Gram–Schmidt are finite formulas. For U⊆VU\subseteq VU⊆V, projection gives V=U⊕U⊥V=U\oplus U^\perpV=U⊕U⊥, best approximation, and Bessel. Finite Riesz gives each φ:V→F\varphi:V\to\Fφ:V→F a unique representative, with the field-dependent ket-to-bra behavior.

theorem
Riesz representation

Let VVV be a finite-dimensional inner-product space over F\FF. For every linear functional φ ⁣:V→F\varphi\colon V\to\Fφ:V→F there is a unique w∈Vw\in Vw∈V with φ(v)=⟨w,v⟩for all v∈V.\varphi(v)=\inner{w}{v}\qquad\text{for all }v\in V.φ(v)=⟨w,v⟩for all v∈V.

remark
Bra–ket duality

Riesz says exactly that the map ∣w⟩↦⟨w∣\ket w\mapsto\bra w∣w⟩↦⟨w∣ is a bijection: every bra (linear functional φ\varphiφ) is ⟨w∣\bra w⟨w∣ for a unique ket ∣w⟩\ket w∣w⟩. The bra space is thus an exact (conjugate-linear) copy of the ket space—the rigorous content of Dirac's notation. The conjugation w=∑kφ(ek)‾ekw=\sum_k\conj{\varphi(e_k)}e_kw=∑k​φ(ek​)​ek​ is the same conjugation that makes ∣ϕ⟩↦⟨ϕ∣\ket\phi\mapsto\bra\phi∣ϕ⟩↦⟨ϕ∣ conjugate-linear over C\CC. Over R\RR this correspondence is linear because conjugation is trivial.

Misconception check

“Projection chooses the nearest basis vector.”

What does projection onto a subspace combine when that subspace has several orthonormal basis vectors?

Reveal the test

It sums every orthogonal component ⟨ek,v⟩ek\langle e_k,v\rangle e_k⟨ek​,v⟩ek​. The answer is generally not one of the basis vectors.

State and explain finite Riesz representation

State finite-dimensional Riesz representation over R\mathbb RR or C\mathbb CC, prove existence and uniqueness, and explain the ket-to-bra scalar convention.

Physics bridge: a projective measurement

A spin in state ∣ψ⟩=12∣0⟩+12∣1⟩\ket\psi=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}\ket0+\tfrac1{\sqrt2}\ket1∣ψ⟩=2​1​∣0⟩+2​1​∣1⟩, measured in the {∣0⟩,∣1⟩}\set{\ket0,\ket1}{∣0⟩,∣1⟩} basis, yields ∣0⟩\ket0∣0⟩ with probability ∣⟨0,ψ⟩∣2=12\abs{\inner0\psi}^2=\tfrac12∣⟨0,ψ⟩∣2=21​ and ∣1⟩\ket1∣1⟩ with probability 12\tfrac1221​. The post-measurement state is the normalized projection onto the observed eigenspace—here ∣0⟩\ket0∣0⟩ or ∣1⟩\ket1∣1⟩. This is the ideal projective (Lueders) measurement model: it uses orthogonal projection followed by renormalization. General measurements need not have this update rule.
