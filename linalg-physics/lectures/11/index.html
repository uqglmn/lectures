↳Linear questions about linear mapsMotivation, conventions, and the finite-dimensional physics setting

We have studied vectors and the operators that move them. This lecture studies the measurements one can make on vectors: the linear functionals φ ⁣:V→F\varphi\colon V\to\Fφ:V→F, which assemble into the dual space V∗V^*V∗. In an inner product space the Riesz theorem of Lecture 8 already tied functionals to vectors—a bra ⟨u∣\bra u⟨u∣ is a dual vector. Here we build the dual abstractly, without an inner product: dual bases, the dual map (the transpose, freed from coordinates), bilinear and multilinear forms, and the covariant/contravariant bookkeeping with Einstein’s summation convention that runs through tensor calculus and relativity. This is Part II of the course: the multilinear algebra underneath tensors.

remarkWhy this mattersOpen the observation

The core is finite-dimensional over R\RR or C\CC. Here V∗=L(V,F)V^*=\Lin(V,\F)V∗=L(V,F) is the algebraic dual. In a complex inner-product space the physics-convention Riesz map V→V∗V\to V^*V→V∗ is conjugate-linear (or linear from the conjugate space); the continuous Hilbert dual is introduced in Lecture 13.

01The dual space
definitionDual space

A linear functional on VVV is a linear map φ ⁣:V→F\varphi\colon V\to\Fφ:V→F. The set of all linear functionals, with pointwise addition and scalar multiplication, is the dual space V∗=L(V,F)V^*=\Lin(V,\F)V∗=L(V,F).

remarkWhy this mattersOpen the observation

If vectors are columns, covectors are rows: φ(v)\varphi(v)φ(v) is the row–column product φ v\varphi\,vφv. If TTT has matrix AAA, a row covector pulls back as φ↦φA\varphi\mapsto\varphi Aφ↦φA. 03Functionals, the Riesz map, and the double dual

A nonzero functional φ\varphiφ has null⁡φ\nul\varphinullφ of dimension n−1n-1n−1: its level sets are parallel hyperplanes, and φ(v)\varphi(v)φ(v) counts (with sign and scale) how many it takes to reach vvv. This is the geometric picture of a covector.

Visual cueOpen the live model
propositionFinite Riesz comparison

Let VVV be a finite-dimensional inner-product space over F\FF. Every algebraic functional φ∈V∗\varphi\in V^*φ∈V∗ has a unique representative u∈Vu\in Vu∈V with φ(v)=⟨u,v⟩\varphi(v)=\inner u vφ(v)=⟨u,v⟩ for all vvv. The map R ⁣:u↦⟨u, ⋅ ⟩R\colon u\mapsto\inner u{\,\cdot\,}R:u↦⟨u,⋅⟩ is linear over R\RR and conjugate-linear over C\CC; equivalently, in the complex case it is a linear isomorphism V‾→V∗\overline V\to V^*V→V∗ from the conjugate vector space.

This is the finite theorem proved in Lecture 8. In Dirac notation R(u)=⟨u∣R(u)=\bra uR(u)=⟨u∣, so a bra acts on ∣v⟩\ket v∣v⟩ to give ⟨u|v⟩\braket u v⟨u∣v⟩. It must not be confused with the canonical double-dual map below: RRR needs an inner product and is conjugate-linear over C\CC, whereas J ⁣:V→V∗∗J\colon V\to V^{**}J:V→V∗∗ needs no metric and is linear over both fields.

04The dual map
