The central thread

The dual space V∗=L(V,F)V^*=\Lin(V,\F)V∗=L(V,F) collects the linear functionals on VVV; a basis eie_iei​ of VVV gives a dual basis eie^iei with ei(ej)=δjie^i(e_j)=\delta^i_jei(ej​)=δji​, and φ(v)=φivi\varphi(v)=\varphi_i v^iφ(v)=φi​vi. In an inner product space Riesz identifies V∗V^*V∗ with VVV (bras are dual vectors), and the double dual V∗∗V^{**}V∗∗ is canonically VVV. A map T ⁣:V→WT\colon V\to WT:V→W has a dual T′ ⁣:W∗→V∗T'\colon W^*\to V^*T′:W∗→V∗, T′φ=φ∘TT'\varphi=\varphi\circ TT′φ=φ∘T, whose matrix is the transpose of TTT's, with null⁡T′=(im⁡T)0\nul T'=(\rng T)^0nullT′=(imT)0. Bilinear and multilinear forms package several vector inputs into a scalar—the determinant being the alternating nnn-linear form. Vector components viv^ivi are contravariant, covector components φi\varphi_iφi​ covariant, summed by Einstein's convention and related through the metric gijg_{ij}gij​—the bookkeeping that tensors (Lecture 12) formalize.

Learn the argument

Let vectors act as measurements

Dual vectors are linear measurements. Their matrices transpose, their bases transform oppositely, and bilinear forms assemble them into tensors.

Misconception check

“A vector and a covector have the same transformation law.”

What must stay invariant when a covector evaluates a vector after a basis change?

Reveal the test

The scalar evaluation must be unchanged, so covector components transform with the inverse rule to vector components.
