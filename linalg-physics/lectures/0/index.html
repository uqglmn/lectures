Start with the seven cold checks

Attempt only the diagnostic route, without notes, and stop after 45 minutes.

  • Add, multiply, conjugate, and divide complex numbers, and move freely between Cartesian and polar form.
  • Multiply matrices, transpose them, and read off the trace, knowing which operations commute and which do not.
  • Evaluate 2×22\times22×2 and 3×33\times33×3 determinants and use det⁡A≠0\det A\neq0detA=0 as the test for invertibility.
  • Row-reduce a linear system, describe its solution set, and find a basis for the solutions of Ax=0Ax=0Ax=0.

The central thread

The four skills above are the computational floor the course is built on. From C\CC you need conjugate, modulus, nonzero division, and the polar form reiθre^{i\theta}reiθ for nonzero numbers, because the course's standard quantum models use complex amplitudes and phases. From matrix algebra you need multiplication (non-commutative!), transpose, and trace. Determinants give the one-number test det⁡A≠0\det A\neq0detA=0 for invertibility and, in Week 5, the characteristic polynomial det⁡(λI−A)\det(\lambda I-A)det(λI−A). Gaussian elimination solves systems and—reading a basis off the RREF of Ax=0Ax=0Ax=0—computes the null spaces, ranks, and dimensions that Lectures 1–4 turn into abstract structure. If the self-check exposes a gap, repair it here before Lecture 1.
