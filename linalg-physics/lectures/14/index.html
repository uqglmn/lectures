The central thread

A bounded operator has finite operator norm and an adjoint defined through the Riesz theorem, and the self-adjoint, unitary, and normal classes of Lecture 9 persist unchanged. The spectrum \spec(T)=\set{\lambda:T-\lambda\idmap\text{ not invertible}} generalizes the eigenvalues: in infinite dimensions it splits into a point spectrum (true eigenvalues) and a continuous spectrum (no eigenvector), with self-adjoint spectra real and unitary spectra on the unit circle. The observables of physics—position x^\hat xx^ and momentum p^=−iℏ d/dx\hat p=-i\hbar\,d/dxp^​=−iℏd/dx—are unbounded and obey [x^,p^]=iℏ\idmap[\hat x,\hat p]=i\hbar\idmap[x^,p^​]=iℏ\idmap, which a trace argument shows is impossible in finite dimensions. Compact self-adjoint operators diagonalize exactly as Hermitian matrices do, T=∑nλn∣en⟩⟨en∣T=\sum_n\lambda_n\ketbra{e_n}{e_n}T=∑n​λn​∣en​⟩⟨en​∣; the general spectral theorem replaces the sum by an integral ∫λ dP(λ)\int\lambda\,dP(\lambda)∫λdP(λ) against a projection-valued measure, and Stone's theorem turns a self-adjoint Hamiltonian into unitary time evolution e−iHt/ℏe^{-iHt/\hbar}e−iHt/ℏ. This is the mathematics of quantum mechanics, and the end of our fourteen lectures.

  • 03Unbounded observables—x^\hat xx^ and p^=−iℏ d/dx\hat p=-i\hbar\,d/dxp^​=−iℏd/dx obey [x^,p^]=iℏ\idmap[\hat x,\hat p]=i\hbar\idmap[x^,p^​]=iℏ\idmap, impossible in finite dimensions (a trace argument).
