The central thread

The adjoint T†T^{\adj}T†, defined by ⟨T†u,v⟩=⟨u,Tv⟩\inner{T^{\adj}u}{v}=\inner{u}{Tv}⟨T†u,v⟩=⟨u,Tv⟩, is the conjugate transpose in an orthonormal basis and obeys (ST)†=T†S†(ST)^{\adj}=T^{\adj}S^{\adj}(ST)†=T†S†, (T†)†=T(T^{\adj})^{\adj}=T(T†)†=T, with null⁡T†=(im⁡T)⊥\nul T^{\adj}=(\rng T)^\perpnullT†=(imT)⊥. Self-adjoint operators (T†=TT^{\adj}=TT†=T) have real eigenvalues and orthogonal eigenvectors—the observables. Unitary operators (U†=U−1U^{\adj}=U^{-1}U†=U−1) preserve inner products and norms, map orthonormal bases to orthonormal bases, and have eigenvalues on the unit circle—the unitary symmetries and closed-system evolutions. Antiunitary symmetries are a separate conjugate-linear possibility. Normal operators (T†T=TT†T^{\adj}T=TT^{\adj}T†T=TT†) satisfy ∥Tv∥=∥T†v∥\norm{Tv}=\norm{T^{\adj}v}∥Tv∥=​T†v​, share eigenvectors with T†T^{\adj}T† (conjugate eigenvalues), and have orthogonal eigenvectors for distinct eigenvalues; in finite-dimensional complex spaces they are exactly the operators the spectral theorem diagonalizes in an orthonormal basis.

Shared eigenvectors with the adjoint

If TTT is normal, then Tv=λvTv=\lambda vTv=λv if and only if T†v=λ‾vT^{\adj}v=\conj\lambda vT†v=λv. Consequently eigenvectors of TTT for distinct eigenvalues are orthogonal.
