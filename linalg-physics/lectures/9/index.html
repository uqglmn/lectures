The central thread

The adjoint T†T^{\adj}T†, defined by ⟨T†u,v⟩=⟨u,Tv⟩\inner{T^{\adj}u}{v}=\inner{u}{Tv}⟨T†u,v⟩=⟨u,Tv⟩, is the conjugate transpose in an orthonormal basis and obeys (ST)†=T†S†(ST)^{\adj}=T^{\adj}S^{\adj}(ST)†=T†S†, (T†)†=T(T^{\adj})^{\adj}=T(T†)†=T, with null⁡T†=(im⁡T)⊥\nul T^{\adj}=(\rng T)^\perpnullT†=(imT)⊥. Self-adjoint operators (T†=TT^{\adj}=TT†=T) have real eigenvalues and orthogonal eigenvectors—the observables. Unitary operators (U†=U−1U^{\adj}=U^{-1}U†=U−1) preserve inner products and norms, map orthonormal bases to orthonormal bases, and have eigenvalues on the unit circle—the symmetries. Normal operators (T†T=TT†T^{\adj}T=TT^{\adj}T†T=TT†) satisfy ∥Tv∥=∥T†v∥\norm{Tv}=\norm{T^{\adj}v}∥Tv∥=​T†v​, share eigenvectors with T†T^{\adj}T† (conjugate eigenvalues), and have orthogonal eigenvectors for distinct eigenvalues; they are the operators the spectral theorem diagonalizes in an orthonormal basis.

definition
Adjoint

Let T∈L(V)T\in\Lin(V)T∈L(V) on a finite-dimensional inner product space. The adjoint T†T^{\adj}T† is the unique operator satisfying ⟨T†u,v⟩=⟨u,Tv⟩for all u,v∈V.\inner{T^{\adj}u}{v}=\inner{u}{Tv}\qquad\text{for all }u,v\in V.⟨T†u,v⟩=⟨u,Tv⟩for all u,v∈V.

The adjoint is the conjugate transpose

In an orthonormal basis, M(T†)=M(T)†\Mat(T^{\adj})=\Mat(T)^{\adj}M(T†)=M(T)†: the matrix of the adjoint is the conjugate transpose of the matrix of TTT.

Self-adjoint

T∈L(V)T\in\Lin(V)T∈L(V) is self-adjoint (Hermitian) if T†=TT^{\adj}=TT†=T, i.e.\ ⟨Tu,v⟩=⟨u,Tv⟩\inner{Tu}{v}=\inner{u}{Tv}⟨Tu,v⟩=⟨u,Tv⟩ for all u,vu,vu,v. Its matrix in an orthonormal basis is a Hermitian matrix, A†=AA^{\adj}=AA†=A.

Misconception check

“Self-adjoint means the matrix is symmetric.”

Over C\CC, which operation must accompany transposition?

Reveal the test

The entries must also be conjugated: self-adjoint means A†=A‾T=AA^\dagger=\overline A^{\mathsf T}=AA†=AT=A.
