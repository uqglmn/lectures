The central thread

The spectral theorem says a normal operator on a complex space has an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors—equivalently a diagonal matrix in an orthonormal basis, T=UDU†T=UDU^{\adj}T=UDU† with UUU unitary. Self-adjoint operators are the special case with real eigenvalues (and a real orthogonal eigenbasis when the space is real), the principal-axes theorem. Grouping eigenspaces gives the spectral decomposition T=∑kλkPkT=\sum_k\lambda_kP_kT=∑k​λk​Pk​ with orthogonal projections satisfying PjPk=δjkPkP_jP_k=\delta_{jk}P_kPj​Pk​=δjk​Pk​ and ∑kPk=I\sum_kP_k=I∑k​Pk​=I, whence f(T)=∑kf(λk)Pkf(T)=\sum_kf(\lambda_k)P_kf(T)=∑k​f(λk​)Pk​. In physics this is the statement that every observable has a complete orthonormal set of real-eigenvalue eigenstates; measurement projects onto them with Born probabilities, and commuting observables diagonalize together. Under a perturbation H0+εVH_0+\varepsilon VH0​+εV the spectrum moves by at most ∣ε∣∥V∥\abs\varepsilon\norm V∣ε∣∥V∥, and the shift of a simple level is computed order by order by Rayleigh–Schr\"odinger, each correction being a matrix element of VVV over a spectral gap; a degenerate level instead splits, by the eigenvalues of VVV compressed into its eigenspace.
