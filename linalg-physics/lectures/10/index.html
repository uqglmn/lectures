The central thread

The finite spectral theorem says a normal operator on a finite-dimensional complex space has an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors—equivalently a diagonal matrix in an orthonormal basis, T=UDU†T=UDU^{\adj}T=UDU† with UUU unitary. Self-adjoint operators are the special case with real eigenvalues (and a real orthogonal eigenbasis when the space is real), the principal-axes theorem. Grouping eigenspaces gives the spectral decomposition T=∑kλkPkT=\sum_k\lambda_kP_kT=∑k​λk​Pk​ with orthogonal projections satisfying PjPk=δjkPkP_jP_k=\delta_{jk}P_kPj​Pk​=δjk​Pk​ and ∑kPk=I\sum_kP_k=I∑k​Pk​=I, whence f(T)=∑kf(λk)Pkf(T)=\sum_kf(\lambda_k)P_kf(T)=∑k​f(λk​)Pk​. In the finite quantum model this supplies real spectral values and eigenspace projectors. The separate Born and Lueders postulates give ideal-projective probabilities and conditional updates, and commuting observables diagonalize together. Continuous spectra and generalized measurements are deferred to Lecture 14.

theorem
Spectral theorem for self-adjoint operators

A self-adjoint operator on a finite-dimensional inner product space (real or complex) has an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors, with all eigenvalues real. Equivalently, a Hermitian matrix factors as A=UDU†A=UDU^{\adj}A=UDU† with UUU unitary and DDD real diagonal; a real symmetric matrix factors as A=ODOTA=ODO^{\T}A=ODOT with OOO orthogonal.

Spectral decomposition

Misconception check

“Every diagonalizable operator has an orthonormal eigenbasis.”

Which extra condition characterises unitary diagonalisation over a complex inner-product space?

Reveal the test

The operator must be normal. Ordinary diagonalisation may use a non-orthogonal eigenbasis.

Explain finite spectral decomposition

From a finite orthonormal eigenbasis, derive T=∑kλkPkT=\sum_k\lambda_kP_kT=∑k​λk​Pk​ for distinct eigenvalues and explain how this computes f(T)f(T)f(T).

Reveal the reasoning rubric

Rubric: group basis vectors by eigenspace; define the mutually orthogonal higher-rank projections PkP_kPk​; show ∑kPk=I\sum_kP_k=I∑k​Pk​=I and TPk=λkPkTP_k=\lambda_kP_kTPk​=λk​Pk​; sum to obtain T=∑kλkPkT=\sum_k\lambda_kP_kT=∑k​λk​Pk​; then apply fff on each eigenspace to get f(T)=∑kf(λk)Pkf(T)=\sum_k f(\lambda_k)P_kf(T)=∑k​f(λk​)Pk​.

Prove simultaneous diagonalization for observables

Prove that commuting finite-dimensional self-adjoint operators A,BA,BA,B have a common orthonormal eigenbasis.

Reveal the reasoning rubric

Rubric: diagonalize AAA; show BBB preserves every AAA-eigenspace from AB=BAAB=BAAB=BA; check the restriction of BBB remains self-adjoint; apply the finite self-adjoint theorem inside each eigenspace; unite the orthonormal block bases.

Separate theorem from measurement postulates

What does the finite spectral theorem supply, and what extra assumptions produce probabilities and conditional states?

Reveal the reasoning rubric

Rubric: the theorem supplies real eigenvalues and orthogonal eigenspace projectors for a self-adjoint matrix. The Born rule Pr⁡(λk)=∥Pkψ∥2\Pr(\lambda_k)=\|P_k\psi\|^2Pr(λk​)=∥Pk​ψ∥2 and the normalized Lueders update Pkψ/∥Pkψ∥P_k\psi/\|P_k\psi\|Pk​ψ/∥Pk​ψ∥ for nonzero-probability outcomes are additional physical postulates of the ideal-projective model, not consequences of diagonalization.

Key concepts5 ideas to retain
  • 01Schur's theorem—every finite-dimensional complex operator is upper-triangular in some orthonormal basis.
  • 02Complex spectral theorem—in finite dimension, normal   ⟺  \iff⟺ orthonormal eigenbasis   ⟺  \iff⟺ unitarily diagonalizable (T=UDU†T=UDU^{\adj}T=UDU†).
  • 03Real spectral theorem—in finite dimension, self-adjoint gives a real orthonormal eigenbasis (principal axes) and real eigenvalues.
  • 04Spectral decomposition—the finite sum T=∑kλkPkT=\sum_k\lambda_kP_kT=∑k​λk​Pk​ has PjPk=δjkPkP_jP_k=\delta_{jk}P_kPj​Pk​=δjk​Pk​, ∑kPk=I\sum_kP_k=I∑k​Pk​=I; f(T)=∑kf(λk)Pkf(T)=\sum_kf(\lambda_k)P_kf(T)=∑k​f(λk​)Pk​.
  • 05Finite projective model—the theorem supplies projectors; Born probabilities and the Lueders update are separate postulates; commuting observables diagonalize together.
