The central thread

A linear map preserves linear combinations, hence superposition. It is fixed by its values on a basis, which may be chosen freely (see the lecture notes). Its kernel null⁡T\nul TnullT and image im⁡T\rng TimT are subspaces; TTT is injective exactly when null⁡T={ 0 }\nul T=\set0nullT={0} and surjective exactly when im⁡T=W\rng T=WimT=W. The rank–nullity theorem dim⁡V=dim⁡null⁡T+dim⁡im⁡T\dim V=\dim\nul T+\dim\rng TdimV=dimnullT+dimimT ties the two together, yielding at a glance that maps to smaller spaces cannot be injective, maps to larger spaces cannot be surjective, and operators on a finite-dimensional space are injective if and only if surjective. In coordinates this is the bookkeeping of pivots and free variables behind every linear system.

Learn the argument

From preserving combinations to counting dimensions

Follow the chain below. Each idea answers a different question about a map, but together they produce rank–nullity.

01

Preserve the structure

Linearity is one rule in disguise

A linear map preserves every linear combination. Once its values are known on a basis, its value on every vector is forced.

definition

Linear map

Let V,WV,WV,W be vector spaces over the same field F\FF. A function T ⁣:V→WT\colon V\to WT:V→W is a linear map (or linear transformation) if T(u+v)=Tu+TvandT(av)=a Tvfor all u,v∈V, a∈F. T(u+v)=Tu+Tv \quad\text{and}\quad T(av)=a\,Tv \qquad\text{for all }u,v\in V,\ a\in\F. T(u+v)=Tu+TvandT(av)=aTvfor all u,v∈V, a∈F. The set of all linear maps V→WV\to WV→W is written L(V,W)\Lin(V,W)L(V,W). A linear map from VVV to itself is an operator, and we write L(V)\Lin(V)L(V) for L(V,V)\Lin(V,V)L(V,V).

03

Count what remains

The domain splits into lost and visible dimensions

Rank–nullity is a conservation law for dimensions: every independent input direction is either lost in the kernel or contributes a direction to the image.

theorem

Rank–nullity

Suppose VVV is finite-dimensional and T∈L(V,W)T\in\Lin(V,W)T∈L(V,W). Then im⁡T\rng TimT is finite-dimensional and dim⁡V=dim⁡null⁡T+dim⁡im⁡T. \dim V=\dim\nul T+\dim\rng T . dimV=dimnullT+dimimT.

A projection makes the count visible

Transfer beyond matrices

Rank and nullity belong to the map, not its notation

Worked example

Rank–nullity without matrices

On P3(R)\Poly_3(\R)P3​(R), which has dimension 444, let DDD be differentiation. Its kernel is the constants, so dim⁡null⁡D=1\dim\nul D=1dimnullD=1; its range is all derivatives of cubics, namely P2(R)\Poly_2(\R)P2​(R), of dimension 333. The theorem checks out: 4=1+34=1+34=1+3. The same DDD is neither injective nor surjective as an operator on P3(R)\Poly_3(\R)P3​(R), yet becomes surjective when viewed as a map P3(R)→P2(R)\Poly_3(\R)\to\Poly_2(\R)P3​(R)→P2​(R): rank and nullity are intrinsic to the map, but injectivity and surjectivity depend on the chosen domain and target.
