The central thread

A linear map preserves linear combinations, hence superposition. It is fixed by its values on a basis, which may be chosen freely (see the lecture notes). Its kernel null⁡T\nul TnullT and image im⁡T\rng TimT are subspaces; TTT is injective exactly when null⁡T={ 0 }\nul T=\set0nullT={0} and surjective exactly when im⁡T=W\rng T=WimT=W. The rank–nullity theorem dim⁡V=dim⁡null⁡T+dim⁡im⁡T\dim V=\dim\nul T+\dim\rng TdimV=dimnullT+dimimT ties the two together, yielding at a glance that maps to smaller spaces cannot be injective, maps to larger spaces cannot be surjective, and operators on a finite-dimensional space are injective if and only if surjective. In coordinates this is the bookkeeping of pivots and free variables behind every linear system.
